The local Grave Warden of [[The South Graveyard]] and leader of the [[Druids of the Pall]].

### Overview

- **Race**: Firbolg.
- **Age**: Upper-40s (arrived in Growm at age 20)
- **Role**: Cemetery keeper. He maintains a quiet, wholesome vigil over the dead, ensuring they rest peacefully—especially on [[Tombs Day]].
- **Home**: A secluded, ranger-like cabin tucked near the edge of the southern pine forests.

# Notes
- **Son/Ward**: [[Arthur Dee]], [[Druids of the Pall|Druid of the Pall]]


# Backstory
Ewen keeps his scars hidden. The scars on his back are a map of his upbringing, a permanent reminder of the thin line he walks between his brutal past and the quiet peace of [[Growm]]. When he speaks of being alive, it isn't with lighthearted ease, but with the heavy gravity of someone who had to fight to understand what that breath actually means.

That struggle began in the Empire under the shadow of his father, a ruthless commander who treated his family like a conquered territory to be broken and forged. Ewen was raised with the expectation of becoming his father and inheriting the position. His father was a monster, terrifyingly smart and utterly devoid of love, who raised Ewen with a militaristic cruelty designed to beat any softness out of him. The agonizing lessons left Ewen’s back permanently ruined, a warning etched onto his body that was never meant as an encouragement, but as a hard-learned, ruthlessly factual lesson of conquest.

His father constantly repeated the phrase "Ubi Vita, Ibi Spes". This translated to: "Where there is life, there is hope." Not as a comforting proverb, but as a warning. On the battlefield, his father used it to justify absolute eradication. He believed that leaving even a single smoldering ember of life in a conquered ruin was a mistake that could cost your life; given enough time, those survivors would clutch the shards of their broken homes, sharpen them on their own pain in the shadows, and strike back with nothing to lose, nothing to fear, and nothing to gain but a shred of hope.

His father whipped this lesson into Ewen's flesh, intending to train a successor who would never hesitate to snuff out a spark. But the warning backfired. In his cruelty, Ewen's father proved a profound truth: the air in a person's lungs is indeed the ultimate weapon of the beaten, a spark capable of eventually toppling empires. His father’s greatest blunder was letting Ewen keep his own tomorrow. When Ewen fled the Empire at eighteen, escaping into a grueling two-year journey of hiding and learning the druidic arts from a mentor on the road, he carried that truth with him like a shield.

By the time Ewen found the mists of Growm, he had completely reframed the doctrine. He realized that hope is not an empty platitude or a danger to be feared, but a fluid, resilient force that changes from day to day. To Ewen, as long as a creature draws air into their lungs, the spark of tomorrow exists, and it is something to be nurtured at all costs. This absolute reverence for a single breath is what drives his quiet vigilance over the valley, and why he didn't hesitate to help those who need him. He knows exactly what a tomorrow is worth.
