**Purpose:** An ancient Green-druid passage through a ridge. The party needs to cross it because the modern route has become impractical; Alexis identifies the old entrance as a potentially usable shortcut. The place has been abandoned for roughly a millennium, and spring has brought its dormant ecosystem roaring back to life.

**Theme:** Stone architecture being reclaimed by vigorous plant life. Nothing is corrupted yet. If anything feels strange, it’s because life here is flourishing _too well_.

---

## Room 1 — Guardian: The Rootbound Gate

The old road disappears into a hillside beneath an arch of weathered green-gray stone. The entrance is open, but spring growth has completely invaded the chamber beyond. Roots snake between paving stones, moss covers old benches, and tiny white flowers grow from cracks in the walls.

**Read aloud:**

> The old trail ends at a broad stone arch half-swallowed by the hillside. Beyond it lies a chamber untouched by any traveler in living memory. Thick roots have buckled the paving stones, and moss hangs from the ceiling in curtains. Along the walls stand rows of stone benches beneath carvings worn nearly smooth by time.
> 
> At the far end, daylight gives way to darkness beneath another archway. Something large has built a nest directly across it. Twigs, bones, leaves, and scraps of ancient cloth form a mound nearly five feet high.
> 
> As you step farther inside, the mound moves.

A **Giant Badger** has made the entrance its den and has **two young** inside. It isn't evil and doesn't immediately charge. It growls, plants itself between the party and its young, and attacks if they continue approaching.

This gives the party a choice. They can fight it, lure it away with food, use Animal Handling, find some clever method around it, or simply retreat. **DC 12 Animal Handling** recognizes that it's defensive rather than predatory; **DC 13** with food or another sensible approach can get it to tolerate their passage.

If they kill it, that's fine. Don't moralize about it. The important information is simply that wildlife is already reclaiming these ancient spaces.

Past the nest is the Green symbol: **a hand beneath a spreading leaf**.

## Room 2 — Puzzle: The Waykeeper's Crossing

This room establishes that the Greenway was infrastructure rather than a temple.

**Read aloud:**

> Beyond the animal den, the tunnel widens into a circular chamber. A deep fissure splits the floor from wall to wall, dropping into darkness below. Whatever bridge once crossed it is gone.
> 
> Four stone pillars rise beside the chasm. Each bears a different carving: a **leaf**, a **flower**, a **root**, and a **thorn**. Across the gap, an old stone doorway waits beneath another carving of the open hand and leaf.
> 
> On your side of the chasm, four flat stones protrude from the wall beneath a faded inscription:
> 
> _“From seed to shelter, walk the Keeper's path.”_

The four symbols are the beginning of a recurring Green trail-language:

**Flower = water/resources. Leaf = passage. Root = shelter. Thorn = danger.**

For this puzzle, there are four stepping stones hidden inside the wall. Pressing the symbols in the natural growth order **Root → Leaf → Flower → Thorn** causes stone slabs to extend across the fissure one at a time.

You don't need to be strict about the exact solution. If the players come up with a reasonable interpretation of “seed to shelter,” reward their reasoning. This is primarily here to introduce the symbols.

A wrong answer causes roots to lash harmlessly from the wall and reset the puzzle. If you want a consequence, the character pressing the symbol makes a **DC 11 Dexterity save** or takes **1 piercing damage** from thorns.

On the other side, they find a scratched message left by some ancient traveler:

> _“Thorn means danger. Learned that one myself. — Edric”_

Now they know at least one symbol for certain.

## Room 3 — Setback: The Lost Trail

This is where the straightforward dungeon route goes wrong.

**Read aloud:**

> The passage beyond slopes gently upward before opening into what must once have been a travelers' hall. Stone tables still stand along the walls, and the remains of an enormous carved map cover the floor.
> 
> But the far half of the chamber is simply gone.
> 
> A section of the mountain has collapsed through it, leaving tons of broken stone where the Greenway once continued. Water trickles between the rocks, feeding a dense garden of ferns and brilliant green shoots growing from the rubble.
> 
> Beside the collapse, someone carved a single symbol into the wall:
> 
> **Root.**

The main route is permanently blocked. The party has to find another way.

This is **Alexis's room**. The Root symbol means shelter, and careful examination reveals an old maintenance passage behind one of the sleeping alcoves. Alexis can also notice airflow coming through the wall or tracks from small animals disappearing behind it.

Don't gate progression behind a check, but checks give advantages. **DC 11 Survival** finds the alternate passage quickly. **DC 13 Investigation** discovers a concealed stone latch. Otherwise, after some searching, they find it anyway.

The important discovery is the **floor map**.

It depicts this region before the winter. Rivers, valleys, trails, and shelters appear where modern maps show nothing. Alexis immediately understands that while much of it will now be inaccurate, some of these routes may exist again. As a mountain guide, she's looking at information nobody alive has possessed for centuries.

Tourmaline can copy it if she wants. Alexis can identify one useful old route that may help the expedition later.

Then they squeeze into the maintenance tunnel.

## Room 4 — Climax: The Keeper's Garden

The cramped tunnel emerges onto a ledge overlooking an underground garden.

This is your big visual and your main fight.

**Read aloud:**

> Warm, damp air brushes across your face as the narrow passage opens suddenly into a cavern.
> 
> Green fills nearly every inch of it.
> 
> Ferns taller than a person crowd around stone walkways. Vines climb columns and disappear into cracks high overhead. Pale blue flowers glow faintly beneath broad leaves, and somewhere beyond the foliage you hear the steady trickle of running water.
> 
> At the center of the cavern stands an enormous tree growing from a circular stone planter. Its trunk has split the masonry apart, its branches reaching toward shafts of sunlight piercing through cracks in the mountain above.
> 
> For the first time since entering the Greenway, you hear birdsong.
> 
> Then one of the shrubs beside you unfolds its legs.

This garden was a **seed and plant repository** maintained by Green's followers. Spring has awakened basically everything simultaneously.

For the fight, use **4 Twig Blights and 2 Needle Blights**. They aren't servants sent by Green and aren't corrupted; they're simply plant creatures that have become territorial after awakening.

Give the room useful terrain. A character can overturn an irrigation channel with an action, creating a 10-foot patch of difficult terrain. Characters can climb onto the old planters for elevation. Dense foliage provides half cover in a few places.

Most importantly, Walthas recognizes plants here that he knows only from old stories and descriptions. He came into the Peaks specifically to document newly returning plants, animals, relics, and texts. This isn't one extinct plant.

It's **dozens**.

For Walthas, this room is potentially one of the most significant natural discoveries of his lifetime.

After the battle, he finds a weatherproof leather field journal inside a stone cabinet.

It belonged to **Ilyan Mossquill, Waykeeper and Naturalist**.

Don't make Ilyan secretly important. He was just a guy who loved this place.

## Room 5 — Reward/Revelation: Names on the Wall

The final chamber sits immediately before the Greenway's exit.

**Read aloud:**

> The garden path eventually reaches a pair of enormous stone doors. Unlike everything behind you, these remain almost perfectly intact.
> 
> Thousands of names cover the surrounding walls.
> 
> Some are carved carefully in elegant script. Others are little more than scratches. Several have dates. Some include tiny pictures—a mountain, a flower, a crude goat, two hands joined together.
> 
> Between them stands a weathered relief of a robed figure kneeling beneath the branches of a tree. One hand rests against the earth. The other is extended toward a traveler.
> 
> Beneath it, a short inscription remains legible:
> 
> _“May all who pass beneath these mountains emerge beneath open sky.”_
> 
> Below the inscription is a stretch of bare stone.

These are the names of travelers who completed the Greenway.

Give the players a moment with it. Don't tell them to carve their names. The empty space makes the invitation obvious enough.

Nearby they find Ilyan's workroom. His journal confirms that the Greenway was maintained under the authority of the **Green Archdruid**, whose servants maintained roads, shelters, gardens, and safe passages through this region.

That's the entire lore revelation.

No ancient war. No suspicious Red figure. No magical winter explanation. No indication that Green is alive.

Just:

**The Green Archdruid's people once protected travelers here.**

Ilyan's journal mostly contains exactly what Walthas would write: sketches of plants, descriptions of animal behavior, weather observations, amusing stories about travelers, and notes about maintaining the trail. Walthas has essentially discovered a naturalist from a thousand years ago who shared his interests.

One unfinished entry can read:

> _“Spotted three red-crested finches near the southern spring this morning. First mating pair I've seen this high in years. If they're nesting, I should—”_

And that's where the surviving page ends.

Not everything unfinished needs to be ominous.

### Treasure

Keep the rewards practical: **2 Potions of Healing** stored in the waykeeper's emergency supplies, Ilyan's journal, several samples of rare plants, and the partial pre-winter trail map. I'd also let Walthas harvest enough unusual specimens to be genuinely valuable to scholars back home.

The biggest reward, though, is travel. Alexis's examination of the old map reveals a surviving route that gives the party **advantage on their next navigation check** and gets them meaningfully closer to Mountain 1.

When they finally push open the doors:

> Stone grinds against stone, and brilliant afternoon sunlight spills through the widening gap.
> 
> Beyond lies a valley untouched by any map you've ever seen. Forest blankets the slopes below. Streams glitter between stands of young trees, and distant herds move through fields of spring grass.
> 
> Far beyond them, rising above ridge after ridge, stands the first of the Three Masters.
> 
> The old Greenway has done exactly what its builders promised.
> 
> It has brought you safely through the mountain.

**DM purpose:** Room 1 establishes spring's returning wildlife; Room 2 teaches Green's symbols; Room 3 hooks Alexis through lost navigation knowledge; Room 4 hooks Walthas through the rediscovery of the natural world; Room 5 gives the Archdruid a small amount of positive characterization. Nobody learns anything important about the campaign's central mystery yet.
