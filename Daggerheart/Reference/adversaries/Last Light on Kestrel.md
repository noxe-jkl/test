## THE PITCH

They say [[Kestrel]] is dying. Not a quick death, mind you, but a slow bleed into the [[Khol]] – that endless, spooky ocean of nothingness that laps at our shores. We float here, a speck of rock and hope, caught between the blinding day of the [[Glint]] and the absolute, liquid darkness of the Khol. Our "night" ain't like other worlds speak of; it's a line, a creeping tide of pure black that you can watch swallow the land, horizon to horizon. When the Khol is up, the stars are gone, the Glint is gone, and only the whispers from the deep and the glow of our laser pistols keep the dark at bay. Folks here are a tough breed – prospectors, drifters, old believers, and young guns looking to make a mark on a world that’s shrinking. We carved a life out of this dustball, built towns that cling to the light, and delved into the old places, the ruins left by whatever came before. But the Khol is rising, they say, or Kestrel is sinking. The "nights" last longer, and things are stirring in the deep black – things we call Tearrors. Now, add to that the strange energies pulsing from the old ruins, whispers of forgotten gods, and the ever-present struggle for what little light and resources we have left. This ain't no place for the faint of heart. This is Kestrel, and it’s asking for heroes, or maybe just stubborn fools who refuse to let the light die.

### TONE & FEEL

Desperate, Gritty, Mysterious, Hopeful (in a bleak way), Cosmic Horror-tinged Western, Awe-Inspiring (the vastness of Khol/Glint), Lonesome.

### THEMES

Survival at the Edge of Annihilation, The Weight of the Past, Faith vs. Despair, Greed in the Face of Oblivion, The Nature of Light and Darkness, Making a Stand When All Seems Lost.

### TOUCHSTONES

Firefly & Serenity (for the Western-in-space feel and character dynamics), The Dark Tower (for the dying world and mysterious, epic journey), Sunless Sea / Sunless Skies (for the cosmic ocean and eldritch dread), Pitch Black (for the terrifying darkness and creatures within), Deadlands (for the weird western and horror elements).

### OVERVIEW

Kestrel is a small, arid planet precariously balanced on the cosmic boundary between two immense, opposing forces: the Glint and the Khol.

- The Glint: This is the source of Kestrel's "day". It's not a sun in the traditional sense, but an endless expanse of blinding, life-giving light and energy. It’s a realm of warmth, harsh but sustaining. Time in the Glint is measured by the slow crawl of Kestrel's shadow across its own surface.
    
- The Khol: This is the "cosmic ocean" of spooky darkness that defines Kestrel's "night". It behaves like a vast, inky liquid, and Kestrel is said to float upon its surface, or perhaps it’s slowly being submerged. The Khol is utterly devoid of natural light, a place of profound silence punctuated by unsettling whispers and the cries of things best left unnamed. When Kestrel rotates into the Khol, the transition is a stark, visible line of shadow consuming the landscape, leading to an impenetrable blackness.
    
- The Twilight Line: The terminator, the line between Glint and Khol, is a constantly moving frontier. Settlements often cling to this line, experiencing a perpetual twilight, or are built to withstand the harsh extremes of full Glint and full Khol.
    

Life on Kestrel is a struggle. Resources are scarce, primarily concentrated in ancient, alien ruins of a precursor civilization or found in dangerous, Khol-touched zones. The pervasive influence of the Khol seeps into the land and its people, causing mutations, madness, or birthing Tearrors – creatures of shadow and nightmare. Technology is a mishmash of salvaged precursor tech, newly innovated survival gear, and rugged, reliable low-tech solutions, including laser firearms that cut through the gloom.

The doom of Kestrel is a slow, encroaching certainty. Whether the planet is sinking into the Khol, the Khol is rising, or the Glint is fading, the outcome seems inevitable: eventual immersion in the endless dark. This knowledge shapes the beliefs, cultures, and desperations of its inhabitants. Some seek escape, others profit, many simply try to survive, and a few, perhaps foolishly, look for a way to hold back the night.

### HERITAGE & CLASSES

Your characters are individuals forged by this harsh existence. They might be natives who have known no other life, or off-worlders who found themselves stranded or seeking fortune on this dying rock. They are likely skilled survivors, gunslingers, scavengers, mystics who tap into the strange energies of Kestrel, or even fallen idealists. The encroaching darkness and the mysteries of the precursor race are central to the world's conflicts.

#### Communities

All communities are available, but their expression is shaped by Kestrel's unique environment.

- Highborne: On Kestrel, "Highborne" doesn't necessarily mean traditional aristocracy. It could refer to the leaders of well-established settlements who control vital resources like precursor tech, clean water, or Glint-facing territory. They might live in fortified Glint-side cities or command mobile land-trains that perpetually chase the Twilight Line. Their "wealth" is often in survival, not just gold.
    

	- Unique Aspect: Might be descended from the original colonists or a group that discovered a significant precursor ruin, giving them a head start.
    
	- Questions: What vital resource does your community control, and what lengths will they go to protect it? How does the "privilege" of your community manifest in a dying world – better tech, safer homes, or just more light?
    

- Loreborne: These are the keepers of Kestrel's fading history, precursor lore, or the dangerous knowledge of the Khol and its entities. They might be isolated scholars in ancient libraries, engineers trying to understand precursor technology, or even mystics who chart the Khol's currents and the Tearrors' movements.
    

	- Unique Aspect: Perhaps they guard the secrets to activating a piece of precursor technology that could help Kestrel, or knowledge that could hasten its doom.
    
	- Questions: What specific, dangerous knowledge does your community protect? Is this knowledge sought by others, and is it a blessing or a curse?
    

- Orderborne: These communities are built around a strict code, faith, or military structure, essential for survival. They could be disciplined militias protecting settlements from Tearrors, religious cults worshipping the Glint, the Khol, or forgotten gods, or even the organized crews of massive sand-crawlers or Khol-skimming vessels (if such things exist).
    

	- Unique Aspect: Their order might be the only thing holding back utter chaos in their region, or their rigid dogma might be blinding them to new threats or solutions.
    
	- Questions: What specific doctrine or authority defines your community's order? How does this order help you survive, and what are its hidden costs or hypocrisies?
    

- Ridgeborne: On Kestrel, "mountains" might be colossal precursor structures, jagged mesas carved by ancient Glint-winds, or even the petrified remains of truly gargantuan creatures. Ridgeborne communities are masters of verticality and surviving in harsh, exposed environments, perhaps mining rare crystals or living in cliff-side settlements.
    

	- Unique Aspect: They might have a unique understanding of Kestrel's geology or access to resources found only at great heights.
    
	- Questions: What unique dangers or advantages does your high-altitude home provide? Are you closer to the Glint's harshness or the Khol's unsettling whispers?
    

- Seaborne: While there are no traditional seas, the Khol itself is a vast, liquid expanse. "Seaborne" communities might be those who dare to navigate the Khol's surface (or just beneath it) in specialized vessels, perhaps scavenging from whatever sinks into it, or they could be communities living on the "shores" of the Khol – the edge of the Twilight Line. Alternatively, they could live by vast, underground reservoirs.
    

	- Unique Aspect: They might possess rare knowledge of Khol navigation, its strange "currents," or the creatures that dwell just below the surface.
    
	- Questions: What is your community's relationship with the Khol – one of fear, cautious exploration, or grim necessity? What unique skills have you developed from living so close to this cosmic ocean?
    

- Slyborne: In a world of scarcity and desperation, Slyborne communities thrive in the shadows. They are smugglers of rare goods, information brokers dealing in secrets, outlaws preying on Glint-side settlements, or even salvagers who delve into Khol-touched ruins where others fear to tread.
    

	- Unique Aspect: They might have hidden routes through Khol-touched lands or contacts with mysterious entities from the deep.
    
	- Questions: What illicit trade or dangerous service does your community provide? What lines are they willing to cross to survive or profit in Kestrel's twilight?
    

- Underborne: These communities live deep beneath Kestrel's surface, perhaps in precursor-carved tunnels, geothermal vents providing warmth and energy, or vast cave systems. They might be escaping the Khol's direct touch or the Glint's harsh radiation, or they might be mining vital resources.
    

	- Unique Aspect: They could be adapted to low-light conditions and possess knowledge of Kestrel's deep places and the things that slumber there.
    
	- Questions: Why did your community choose to live beneath the surface? What unique dangers and wonders do the deeps of Kestrel hold?
    

- Wanderborne: Nomadic communities are common on Kestrel, constantly moving to find resources, escape threats, or follow the Twilight Line. They might be land-train caravans, tribes following migratory patterns of strange fauna, or pilgrims seeking a mythical safe haven.
    

	- Unique Aspect: Their constant movement gives them a broad, if shallow, understanding of Kestrel's diverse regions and dangers.
    
	- Questions: What forces your community to constantly wander? What unique skills or knowledge have you gained from a life on the move?
    

- Wildborne: Kestrel's "wildernesses" are truly alien – fields of strange, crystal-like flora, petrified forests of unknown origin, or lands twisted by Khol energy or precursor terraforming. Wildborne communities are deeply connected to these strange environments, perhaps as hunters of unique beasts, mystics who draw power from the land, or isolationists.
    

	- Unique Aspect: They might possess unique survival skills adapted to Kestrel's bizarre ecosystems or a resistance to local environmental hazards.
    
	- Questions: What strange wilderness does your community call home? What unique relationship do you have with its alien flora and fauna?
    

#### Ancestries

All ancestries are available, finding their place in the desperate tapestry of Kestrel.

- Clank: Perhaps Clanks are salvaged precursor automatons, custom-built survival mechs, or individuals who have replaced failing flesh with resilient machinery to survive Kestrel’s harshness or the Khol’s touch. Their construction might incorporate scavenged alien materials or hardy Kestrel-grown crystals.
    
- Drakona: Descendants of bio-engineered guardians from the precursor era, or perhaps a species that evolved in Kestrel's unique energy fields. Their scales might offer protection against the Glint's radiation or the Khol's chill, and their elemental breath could be a vital survival tool or weapon.
    
- Dwarf: Master tunnellers and craftspeople, perhaps their communities delved deep to escape the surface dangers, becoming the primary miners of rare ores and energy crystals, or builders of subterranean settlements. Their resilience is invaluable on Kestrel.
    
- Elf: Perhaps Elves on Kestrel are attuned to the strange energies of the precursor ruins, their long lifespans dedicated to deciphering ancient secrets. Their trance might allow them to perceive shifts in the Khol or Glint energies.
    
- Faerie: Native to Kestrel's bizarre ecosystems, or perhaps beings transformed by Khol energy or precursor mutagens. Their connection to strange flora and insectile features might allow them to thrive in Khol-touched zones or communicate with Kestrel's unique wildlife. Their wings could be a vital asset for navigating treacherous terrain.
    
- Faun: Hardy survivalists adapted to Kestrel’s rugged badlands and mesas. Their goat-like agility makes them excellent climbers and scouts in the broken landscapes. They might be nomadic herders of strange Kestrel beasts.
    
- Firbolg: Perhaps gentle giants who seek to protect the last vestiges of Kestrel's natural (or unnatural) beauty, or formidable guardians of sacred precursor sites. Their strength and connection to the land are significant assets.
    
- Fungril: Native to Kestrel's shadowed canyons or damp, Khol-touched caverns. Their mycelial networks might allow communication across distances or even tap into the strange consciousness of the Khol itself.
    
- Galapa: Their shells might offer natural protection against Kestrel's environmental hazards, from Glint radiation to the Khol's chilling touch, or even shrapnel in firefights. They could be slow, methodical archivists of precursor lore or resilient defenders of fortified settlements.
    
- Giant: Perhaps descendants of laborers from a forgotten era, or a people mutated by Kestrel's energies to achieve great size and strength, vital for heavy labor or defense in a hostile world. Their reach could be invaluable in salvage or combat.
    
- Goblin: Master scavengers and tinkerers, adept at surviving in the forgotten underbellies of precursor ruins or the sprawling junk-towns that dot Kestrel. Their keen senses are perfect for spotting salvage or danger in the dim light.
    
- Halfling: Their luck and knack for going unnoticed make them excellent scouts, smugglers, or survivors in Kestrel's dangerous settlements. Their attunement to magnetic fields could be reflavored as an innate sense for precursor energy lines or safe paths through Khol-touched areas.
    
- Human: Adaptable and numerous, humans form the backbone of many settlements, driven by a fierce will to survive and make their mark on this dying world. Their endurance serves them well in Kestrel's harsh climates.
    
- Infernis: Perhaps their ancestry is tied to strange, demonic entities from the Khol itself, or they are a result of precursor experimentation. Their dread visage and resistance to fear could be powerful assets when facing Tearrors.
    
- Katari: Agile hunters and scouts, perfectly adapted to Kestrel's shadows and broken landscapes. Their retractable claws and keen senses make them formidable in close-quarters encounters or when tracking prey (or enemies).
    
- Orc: Known for their sturdiness and imposing presence, Orcs might be frontline defenders of settlements, rugged prospectors, or even enforcers in the more lawless parts of Kestrel. Their tusks could be adorned with precursor artifacts or kill-marks.
    
- Ribbet: Perhaps adapted to Kestrel's few remaining marshlands, Khol-flooded caves, or underground reservoirs. Their amphibious nature could allow them to explore areas inaccessible to others, and their long tongues might be used for scavenging or defense.
    
- Simiah: Natural climbers and explorers of Kestrel's vertical environments, from towering precursor ruins to deep chasms. Their agility and prehensile feet make them uniquely suited for navigating such treacherous places.
    
- Mixed Ancestry: Kestrel is a melting pot of desperate survivors. Mixed ancestries are common, reflecting the blending of peoples and the strange influences of the world itself.
    

### PLAYER PRINCIPLES

- Embrace the Grit: Kestrel is a tough place. Characters will face hardship, scarcity, and moral ambiguity. Don't shy away from the struggle; let it shape your character.
    
- The Light You Carry Matters: In a world literally on the edge of an all-consuming darkness, every act of hope, defiance, or compassion shines brightly. Consider what "light" your character carries – a belief, a person, a goal – and how they protect it.
    
- The Past is Never Dead: The ruins of the precursors and the encroaching Khol are constant presences. Your character is likely affected by both. Explore their relationship with Kestrel's mysteries and its inevitable doom.
    
- Make Your Mark (While There's Still Time): Kestrel might be doomed, but that doesn't mean your actions are meaningless. What legacy will your character try to leave? How will they rage against the dying of the light?
    

### GM PRINCIPLES

- The Khol is a Character: It's not just darkness; it's an oppressive, sentient presence. Describe its inky blackness, its silence, the way it seems to absorb light and sound, the unsettling whispers on the edge of hearing. Let its presence be felt even when it's "day".
    
- Contrast Light and Dark: Make the Glint's light harsh and revealing, a counterpoint to the Khol's mystery. The Twilight Line should be a zone of tension and fleeting beauty. Use the stark transitions to create dramatic shifts in mood and danger.
    
- Lean into the Weird West: This is a frontier at the edge of oblivion. Combine tropes of classic westerns (dusty towns, desperate people, lawlessness, exploration) with science fiction (lasers, precursor tech, alien landscapes) and cosmic horror (Tearrors, the Khol's influence).
    
- Resources are Precious: Emphasize scarcity. Light, clean water, fuel, ammunition, precursor artifacts – these are things people might kill for. Make acquiring and protecting them meaningful.
    
- Hope is a Flickering Flame: Kestrel is a grim place, but without hope, there's no story. Let players find small victories, moments of beauty, or reasons to keep fighting, even if the overall outlook is bleak. The doom is slow, not immediate.
    

### DISTINCTIONS

- The Glint: The Glint is an ever-present, unblinking expanse of pure, intense light that bathes one side of Kestrel. It's not a star, but a boundless stellar phenomenon.
    

- Properties: Provides warmth and the energy for what little photosynthesis occurs in Kestrel's hardy flora. Its raw radiation can be dangerous over long exposures without protection. Time is often measured by the position of Kestrel's own shadow.
    
- Influence: Settlements on the Glint-side are often more established but must contend with the harsh, unceasing light and potential for Glint-scalds or radiation sickness. Glint-facing ruins might contain solar-powered precursor technology.
    

- The Khol: The Khol is a vast, seemingly infinite ocean of liquid darkness that Kestrel perpetually touches. Its true nature is unknown and a source of much superstition and fear.
    

- Properties: Utterly black, absorbing light. It has a tangible, liquid-like quality, with slow currents and "depths". Strange whispers and psychic pressures emanate from it. Direct, prolonged contact is often corrupting or maddening.
    
- Influence: The Khol is the source of the "Tearrors". Its touch can warp landscapes and creatures. Khol-side settlements are often desperate, shadowy places. Scavenging items that have sunk into the Khol is incredibly perilous but can yield unique or valuable finds. The "night" on Kestrel is the period the land is submerged or facing the Khol. The transition is a stark, visible line of shadow sweeping across the planet.
    

- The Twilight Line (The Brink): This is the ever-moving terminator line where Glint meets Khol. Many settlements, known as Brink-Towns, are built here, experiencing a perpetual twilight.
    

- Properties: A zone of dramatic light and shadow, often with unique atmospheric phenomena. Temperatures can fluctuate wildly.
    
- Influence: Brink-Towns are often hubs of trade, smuggling, and desperate hope, as inhabitants try to harness the benefits of both Glint and Khol while mitigating their dangers. Life is a constant adaptation to the shifting light.
    

- Precursor Ruins: Scattered across Kestrel are the enigmatic ruins of an ancient, advanced civilization, referred to only as the Precursors.
    

- Properties: Made of unknown, incredibly durable materials. Often contain dormant or malfunctioning technology, strange energy sources, and cryptic warnings or historical records. Many are Khol-touched or irradiated by the Glint.
    
- Influence: The primary source of advanced technology, rare resources, and dangerous knowledge. Exploring them is perilous but often necessary for survival or advancement. Their original purpose, and the reason for their disappearance, is a central mystery.
    

- Tearrors: These are creatures anathema to light and life, seemingly birthed from or intrinsically linked to the Khol.
    

- Formation: The exact genesis of Tearrors is a mystery. Some believe they are native inhabitants of the Khol, able to manifest on Kestrel when the darkness is deepest. Others whisper they are formed from the fears, despair, or corrupted life-force of those lost to the Khol. Intense negative emotion or psychic trauma near Khol-touched areas are often cited as catalysts for their appearance. A "Tearror Bloom" might occur when a particularly potent source of fear or a Khol-energy surge happens, causing multiple Tearrors to manifest.
    
- Behavior: They vary wildly in form and function, from shadowy stalkers and multi-limbed horrors to psychic entities that prey on sanity. Most are hostile to Glint-born life and are weakened or repelled by intense light.
    

- Laser Technology: Firearms on Kestrel are predominantly energy-based, colloquially known as "lasers," "blasters," or "Glint-guns".
    

- Properties: They fire beams of concentrated light or energy. Ammunition is typically a replaceable energy cell or crystal. Different focusing crystals or power sources can alter a weapon's range, damage type (e.g., burning, concussive, Khol-dampening), and intensity.
    
- Influence: Essential for defense against Tearrors and other threats. The quality and availability of energy cells and focusing crystals are major economic and survival factors. Precursor ruins are a prime source for advanced laser components.
    

- Factions:
    

- The Glint Wardens: An order dedicated to preserving knowledge and technology that maximizes the use of Glint energy and protects against the Khol. Often seen as pragmatic but sometimes dogmatic.
    
- Khol Seekers: Groups and cults that, for various reasons (despair, a thirst for power, a belief in hidden truths), seek to understand or even embrace the Khol. Often viewed with suspicion and fear.
    
- Brink Prospectors Union: A loose affiliation of scavengers, miners, and explorers who brave the dangers of the Twilight Line and nearby ruins for resources. Highly independent and resourceful.
    
- The Unblinking Eye: A secretive information network that deals in rumors, precursor lore, and Khol sightings. Their motives are often unclear.
    

### INCITING INCIDENT

The old Precursor beacon atop Whisper Peak, silent for generations, has begun to emit a faint, rhythmic pulse of light into the Khol during the deepest "night". The pulse is too weak to be a signal, more like a dying heartbeat. Elder Elara, a reclusive Loreborne historian in the Brink-Town of Last Stop, believes it’s a distress call, or perhaps a final warning, from a Precursor automated system deep within the mountain. Simultaneously, Tearror activity around Last Stop has spiked alarmingly since the pulsing began. The local militia is overwhelmed. Elara posts a bounty, not for clearing out Tearrors, but for a party to ascend Whisper Peak, investigate the beacon, and, if possible, decipher its message or silence it before it draws something truly catastrophic from the Khol, or worse, drains Last Stop's dwindling power reserves if it's found to be drawing energy from the town's grid. The journey will be perilous, through Khol-touched territory, facing heightened Tearror threats, and the mystery of the Precursors themselves.

### CUSTOM MECHANICS

- The Khol's Touch (Corruption): Prolonged exposure to the Khol or intense encounters with Tearrors can inflict "Khol-Stain" on characters.
    

- Gaining Khol-Stain:
    

- Taking Severe damage from a Tearror or Khol-aspected environmental hazard.
    
- Failing a roll with Fear while in deep Khol territory or during a highly stressful Khol-related encounter (GM discretion).
    
- Spending extended periods (multiple "nights") in areas heavily suffused with Khol energy.
    

- Effects of Khol-Stain: Each time a character gains a Khol-Stain, they mark it on their character sheet (max 3).
    

- 1 Stain: The character becomes unnerved and suffers disadvantage on Presence rolls when interacting with those untainted by the Khol. They might hear faint whispers or see fleeting shadows.
    
- 2 Stains: The character gains a minor physical Khol-mutation (e.g., patches of inky skin, eyes that reflect no light, a constant chill). They also gain a new, unsettling Experience (e.g., "Khol-Whispers," "Shadow-Skulker," "Void-Sense") starting at +0, chosen with the GM. When they roll with Fear on an action related to the Khol or darkness, they can add this Experience's modifier.
    
- 3 Stains: The character is on the brink of being lost to the Khol. They must make a crucial choice:
    

- Embrace the Dark: They become a Khol-Touched. Their unsettling Experience modifier increases to +2. They gain a unique Khol-gift (e.g., ability to see in absolute darkness, command minor shadow manifestations, increased resilience to Tearrors) but are forever changed, viewed with fear and suspicion, and potentially hunted. They can never truly be free of the Khol's influence. This path might lead to unique story opportunities or a tragic end.
    
- Seek the Light (Cleanse): This requires a significant quest, perhaps finding a potent Precursor artifact, a ritual at a Glint-soaked sanctuary, or a perilous journey into a place of pure light. Success removes all Khol-Stains but may come at a great cost or leave a permanent scar (narrative or mechanical).
    

- Losing Khol-Stain: Besides cleansing at 3 Stains, some rare Precursor artifacts or rituals might allow for the removal of a single Khol-Stain, but these should be difficult and costly endeavors.
    

- Tearror Manifestation: Instead of pre-placing all Tearrors, the GM can use the Khol's influence to manifest them dynamically.
    

- Fear Triggers: When the GM spends Fear, especially in Khol-touched areas or during moments of high tension/fear from the PCs:
    

- 1 Fear: A minor Tearror (Minion-level) manifests from nearby shadows.
    
- 2 Fear: A standard Tearror (Standard or Skulk) emerges, perhaps drawn by the PCs' emotions or the disturbance they're causing.
    
- 3+ Fear: A significant Tearror (Bruiser, Leader, or even Solo if the Fear pool is high and the situation warrants it) tears its way into reality from the Khol.
    

- Khol Surges: At GM discretion, particularly potent Khol events (like the Khol reaching its zenith over an area, or a precursor device interacting negatively with Khol energy) can cause a "Tearror Bloom," where multiple adversaries manifest simultaneously without direct Fear cost, though such an event might grant the GM Fear.
    

- Laser Weapons (Examples): These use standard Daggerheart weapon rules but are re-flavored. Ammunition can be abstracted as "charges" or "energy cells" which might need to be found, bought, or recharged (potentially a downtime activity or resource to track if desired).
