This is a poem she’s never said to anyone. It’s one she reads constantly but keeps hidden from prying eyes saving it for when she meets Rowan again. 
_**The space you left between the lines**_ 
> _I miss you in ways that do not make sense to explain. Not loudly. Not dramatically. I miss you the way a room misses a window after it has been boarded shut—still shaped for light, still reaching for where it remembers warmth. I keep thinking time will make you smaller, that memory will soften your sharp edges until you fit somewhere safer inside me. It hasn’t. You are still exactly the size of my life. They ask what I lost when the banners rose. I tell them nothing I can name. It’s easier that way. There are no words wide enough for the emptiness you left behind. What I lost was the person I measured my days against. The one whose presence made the future feel not just possible, but worth surviving for. I wonder if you miss me—or if choosing your side gave you permission to set me down gently and keep walking. I hope it did. I hope at least one of us gets to be whole, heart unbroken, hands steady. Some nights I imagine we were braver, or crueler, or less in love. Any of those might have saved us. Instead, we were careful. We were honest. We loved each other without reservation and believed love did not require agreement to endure. I still believe that. That is the cruel part. If we meet again, I will reach for you. I will not speak. I will run to you as if no banner ever stood between us, as if my body remembers a truth the world tried to overwrite. But know this—there is no side of this war where I am not missing you, loving you, wanting you here with me, as if choosing you was never something I stopped doing._
