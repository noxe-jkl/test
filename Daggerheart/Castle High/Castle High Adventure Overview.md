# Castle High: The Ninth Missing Student

**A Seven-Session Mini-Series GM Guide**

The central mystery remains the same as the core frame: Professor Vale's attempts to contact the entity known as the Sleeper Above accidentally created a passage to the extradimensional Elsewhere. The eight students who vanished last year became trapped there, Headmaster Fellowes disappeared while investigating Vale, Oliver Fleming has somehow escaped, and Riley has just discovered enough of the truth to put themself directly in danger.

_Note: Each session below assumes roughly 3–4 hours of play._

## Session 1 — The Boy Who Fell From the Clouds

### Purpose

Introduce Castle High, establish Riley as someone the PCs genuinely care about, put the mystery directly in front of them, and make breaking school rules the natural first step of the campaign.

The frame's opening already provides an excellent inciting incident: Oliver Fleming, one of last year's eight missing students, crash-lands in the courtyard, tries to warn everyone, and loses consciousness before finishing his sentence.

### Opening: Last Night of Orientation

Don't begin with the mystery immediately. Give them twenty or thirty minutes to be kids. It's the final evening of orientation. The courtyard is full of students comparing schedules, showing off broom tricks, exchanging phone numbers, gossiping about teachers, and trying to figure out which upperclassmen are cool.

Ask each player one question:

- Who have you already developed a crush on?
    
- Which teacher do you desperately want to impress?
    
- What embarrassing thing happened to you during orientation?
    
- What stupid thing did Riley convince your group to do?
    
- Which other tower group already hates you?
    

Let Riley exist in these scenes. Have Riley steal somebody's fries. Have them complain about tomorrow's schedule. Have them make plans with one PC for next weekend.

Then—something emerges from the clouds. A broom. Someone is hanging from it.

**CRASH.** Oliver Fleming hits the courtyard. He looks directly at one nearby student.

> "They're going to get—"

And collapses. Mrs. Wickett and several teachers rush in. An older student recognizes him. _"That's Oliver Fleming."_ Oliver disappeared last year. Suddenly the courtyard isn't fun anymore.

### Scene 2: Rumor Explosion

The school orders everyone back to their towers. Nobody obeys quietly. Within twenty minutes the student rumor network has produced six completely different explanations.

Use rumors from the frame itself:

- The Night Snatcher took the missing kids.
    
- Headmaster Fellowes made them disappear.
    
- One of the tower deans did it.
    
- They joined a cult.
    
- A dragon ate them.
    
- They discovered something the school wanted hidden.
    

Those kinds of rumors are already circulating canonically. Let the PCs decide what they believe. Riley believes Oliver knows something.

More importantly: **Riley wants to sneak into the infirmary tonight.**

The PCs can agree, refuse, argue, or propose another plan. Before they can act, Riley disappears from the dorm for a few minutes. They return evasive and nervous. If questioned: _"Nothing. I thought I saw someone."_

That's the last conversation the PCs have with Riley.

### Scene 3: Morning

Riley's bed is empty. Their broom is gone. Their backpack is still there. So is their journal.

Headmaster Dove gives a painfully controlled announcement:

> "Riley Averill is currently 'unaccounted for.' Students should not speculate. Nobody should leave campus. Any student found interfering with the faculty investigation will face disciplinary action."

That immediately tells teenagers exactly what they should do.

### Scene 4: Riley's Journal

Use the journal as the campaign's recurring clue-delivery mechanism, just as the frame suggests. Pages can reveal mystery information while also containing Riley's feelings, embarrassing observations, relationship drama, and opinions about the PCs.

Almost every page has been torn out. One remains. Customize its first half using things the players established about Riley during character creation. The important final lines are:

> _Oliver shouldn't have been able to come back._ _I think somebody knew he would._ _I don't know who I can trust yet._ _Going to see Willis. He always calms me down._

Who's Willis? Let the PCs investigate. Eventually someone tells them: Willis is a griffin. He's in the stables.

### Scene 5: Sneaking Out

Now make Castle High itself the dungeon. The PCs need to get from their tower to the Landing after curfew.

Offer several routes:

- Forgotten corridors.
    
- The roof.
    
- Through the library.
    
- Out a window and around the castle on brooms.
    
- Straight through the halls while pretending they have permission.
    

Let their character abilities determine the solution. Along the way, introduce one recurring authority figure—a prefect, teacher, magical hall monitor, or aggressively responsible student. Also let them glimpse something inexplicable: a single light illuminates a window in sealed Lock Tower. It is midnight. That's exactly the rumor associated with Lock Tower. Don't explain it.

### Scene 6: Willis

The Landing is eerie after dark. Griffins rustle in their stalls. The castle's enormous shadow stretches over the clouds. Willis recognizes the PCs because Riley brought them treats during orientation.

Searching his stall reveals something stuffed behind a feed barrel: **Riley's phone.**

The screen is shattered. There are several photographs. The last photograph shows a distant ruin. Riley is visible in reflection. And farther behind them—**Professor Vale**.

Then somebody says: _"Interesting place for a midnight study session."_ Headmaster Dove stands behind them.

_End session._

## Session 2 — Detention at 10,000 Feet

### Purpose

Deliver the first real skyland adventure, introduce Vale properly, establish that the disappearances extend beyond Castle High, and demonstrate that flight is the campaign's signature action language. Castle High expects students to fly and gives each PC a personalized skyshard artifact; the frame encourages flying to be expressive, dangerous, and stylish rather than merely movement.

### Opening: Headmaster's Office

Dove interrogates them. Why were they out? What did they find? Did Riley tell them anything?

Let the PCs decide how much they reveal. Dove confiscates Riley's phone if they know about it. If the PCs successfully conceal it, great. Either way, Dove says something revealing:

> _"Whatever happened to Riley, you are not going after them."_

_Not if something happened. Whatever happened._

Then comes punishment. Saturday detention. Their tower grade takes a hit too. Their rivals will be delighted.

### School Montage

Before Saturday, show consequences. A teacher asks why they're falling asleep in class. Someone has made "RILEY LIVES" stickers. Other students ask the PCs invasive questions because Riley was their roommate. Someone claims Riley ran away voluntarily.

One PC receives an anonymous text:

> **STOP LOOKING.**

Nobody can trace the number.

### Detention

Their detention supervisor is **Professor Vale**.

Vale should immediately complicate expectations. They're funny. They're attentive. They're an excellent teacher. They remember everyone's name. They ask sincerely how the PCs are coping with Riley's disappearance.

Vale explains that detention today involves useful labor: retrieving lost school equipment from a debris field Castle High is passing. They're going flying.

### The Debris Field

This is your first aerial playground. Floating doors. Chunks of masonry. An upside-down bell tower. A broken skyship rotating slowly through the clouds. A bathtub. Several extremely confused sheep.

Encourage the PCs to show off their flight styles. Give them opportunities for races and tricks before anything dangerous happens. Then someone discovers: **an abandoned Castle High broom.**

Vale freezes. Only for a moment. It belonged to one of last year's missing students. Before anyone can investigate—something enormous passes beneath them.

### The Dragonshark Chase

A **dragonshark** erupts from the clouds. Do not frame this as "roll initiative and kill it." It's vastly too dangerous. The question is: _How do you escape?_

Thread through wreckage. Dive toward the cloud layer. Use floating debris as cover. Distract it. Save a classmate whose artifact breaks. Let each PC's flight style matter. The frame explicitly encourages aerial threats that are too dangerous to fight directly and recommends simply telling players when something starts chasing them. Eventually they escape.

### The Broom

Back at the debris field—or after somebody grabbed it during the chase—they examine the missing student's broom. Inside a compartment is something Riley hid recently: another journal page.

Riley had been tracking all eight disappearances. Each student disappeared on a different skyland. But those skylands share one feature: Every one contains ancient ruins built around unusually large skyshards.

Riley writes:

> _Somebody at school knows what these places are._ _I think the library does too._ _Ask the ghost._

Vale appears behind them. They look at the page. Their expression becomes impossible to read. Then: _"Did Riley leave anything else?"_

_End session._

## Session 3 — Secrets in the Stacks

### Purpose

Make investigation personal, reveal Vale's history, introduce forbidden student culture, and transform the mystery from "something is kidnapping students" into "this has happened before."

### Opening: Normal School Is Becoming Impossible

Start with classes. The PCs have an assignment due. Their tower grade is suffering because of their adventures. Their rival group publicly humiliates them. One PC gets asked to the upcoming Founder's Festival.

Another teacher pulls someone aside: _"You're talented. Don't throw away your year chasing rumors."_

This stuff matters. The players should feel that solving Riley's disappearance is actively destroying their ordinary lives.

### The Library Ghost

Castle High's library is ancient, contains a possibly even-more-ancient computer room, and is rumored to contain a ghost who exchanges secrets for secrets. The rumor is true.

Call her **Marnie Quill**. She died at Castle High decades ago and has spent the afterlife reading everybody's returned library books. She knows everything. Unfortunately, Marnie never gives information away. _Secret for secret._

Ask each PC for something their character has genuinely never told the others. Make the revelations meaningful. Then Marnie gives them hers:

> _"Professor Vale disappeared once too."_

Vale was a Castle High student. They disappeared for eleven days. Then returned. They claimed they'd gotten lost on a skyland. Nobody believed them.

### Old Records

The PCs can research Vale. This gives different PCs opportunities to contribute: Magical research. Breaking into restricted records. Sweet-talking the librarian. Using an ancient computer that sounds like a lawn mower.

Eventually they find an old photograph: Teenage Vale. Teenage Fellowes. And several other students. Behind them: the same ruins visible in Riley's phone. One student in the photograph has been scratched out.

### The Midnight Class

Marnie points them toward the Forgotten Corridors. Castle High students hold secret Midnight courses there, outside the official curriculum, and older students circulate an incomplete map of the corridors.

Tonight's class: **Unauthorized Dimensional Theory**. It's run by an absurdly confident senior who absolutely does not understand half of what they're teaching. This gives you some comedy.

Then somebody notices the PCs' map. _"Where'd you get that?"_ Riley drew part of it. Specifically, Riley added a corridor nobody else knew existed.

### Rivals

The PCs' rival group arrives. They've been investigating Riley too. But they have reached a different conclusion: the PCs know more than they're admitting. Maybe Riley argued with one PC shortly before disappearing. Maybe someone saw the group snooping around.

Turn this into an emotionally charged confrontation. It can become a duel. It can become a shouting match. It can become an alliance. Then—faculty patrol. Both groups must flee together.

### The Hidden Room

Following Riley's addition to the map leads to a door behind a wall. Inside are nine photographs: Oliver Fleming. Seven other missing students. Riley.

Each photograph is attached to maps, magical readings, weather charts and skyshard measurements. Eight photographs have a black slash through them. Riley's does not.

There's also a tenth photograph: Headmaster Fellowes. Underneath:

> **SUBJECT F — CROSSING SUCCESSFUL.**

Someone has been experimenting on them. Then the door closes behind the PCs. Professor Vale's voice comes from the darkness.

> _"You really shouldn't be here."_

_Cut._

## Session 4 — Happyland After Dark

### Purpose

Reveal that the missing students may still be alive, give the PCs their biggest adventure yet, and blur the distinction between Vale as villain and Vale as desperate rescuer.

### Opening: Vale's Explanation

Vale doesn't attack. Instead: _"You're asking the right questions much too quickly."_

Vale claims the room belonged to Fellowes. They claim Fellowes was investigating the disappearances. Vale helped. Then Fellowes disappeared too. Vale refuses to explain "crossing successful."

Before leaving, they tell the PCs: _"If Riley left you clues, follow them. But don't trust Dove."_ Now the players have competing conspiracies. Good.

### Teenage Problem of the Week

Founder's Festival is approaching. The PCs have obligations. Someone needs a date. Someone promised to perform. Their tower has a project. Their rivals want a rematch. Spend some time here before returning to mystery.

Then the next journal page appears somewhere impossible—perhaps inside a PC's textbook. It contains coordinates. And:

> _Happyland. Midnight._ _That's where I first saw it._

### Happyland Coaster Park

The frame gives you a nearly perfect teenage adventure location: an abandoned sky amusement park with rusting roller coasters, carnival games, slides dropping off the island, and a Ferris wheel with an unblinking eye. Students already use it for secret meetings.

Naturally: There's an illegal Castle High party happening there. Let them have fun. Give them a ridiculous enchanted carnival game. Let them ride a broken roller coaster using magic to keep it on the track. Let relationship drama happen. Let somebody kiss somebody.

Then at midnight: every ride activates simultaneously. The music stops. The Ferris wheel looks directly at the PCs.

### Beneath Happyland

Riley's coordinates point below the haunted house. There is an ancient chamber underneath it. Its architecture matches the ruins in Vale's photograph. A dormant machine surrounds a massive skyshard.

The PCs activate it. A magical recording plays. Fellowes and Vale. They're arguing.

> **Fellowes:** "Eight children, Vale." **Vale:** "Eight children who are still alive." **Fellowes:** "You cannot know that." **Vale:** "I heard them." **Fellowes:** "If you open it again, whatever is on the other side may hear us too."

Recording ends. That's when the storm hits.

### Happyland Falls Apart

A magical storm engulfs the island. Gravity shifts. Roller coaster tracks tear free. The Ferris wheel walks. Yes. _The Ferris wheel walks._

Run an enormous escape sequence. During it, one PC flies through a shimmering distortion. For five seconds: Silence. Black sunlight. A castle floating upside down. Eight distant figures. And Riley. Alive.

Riley sees them. Runs toward them. Screams:

> _"DON'T LET VALE OPEN IT!"_

The PC snaps back into reality. Happyland explodes behind them. Now they know. Riley is alive.

## Session 5 — The Castle Cup

### Purpose

Put the supernatural plot and school plot directly on top of one another. This should be the most "high school movie" session. Castle High's Star Tower is explicitly built around multi-event competition, giant scoreboards, rivalries, and teamwork, so even if the PCs aren't Star students, borrowing that culture for an inter-tower Castle Cup fits the setting naturally.

### The Problem

The distortion Riley appeared through can be observed. But only through Castle High's mounted telescope. The telescope is on the Ramparts, and today it's reserved for faculty and the winners of the Castle Cup astronomy challenge. So the PCs need to do something humiliating. Participate in school activities.

### The Castle Cup

Run several short challenges.

- **Trial One: The Gauntlet** - Obstacle course through floating rings, moving statues and magical hazards. Pure flight.
    
- **Trial Two: Practical Magic** - Teams receive a bizarre magical problem. Example: _"This owlbear has swallowed an enchanted alarm clock. Remove it without hurting the owlbear."_ Don't determine a solution. Let the PCs invent one.
    
- **Trial Three: Duel** - Nonlethal magical combat. Let a PC fight their rival. Make the emotional stakes more important than the mechanical ones.
    
- **Final: Sky Relay** - A race around Castle High. Brooms. Flying swords. Griffins. Cheating. Spectators screaming. Perfect.
    

Then midway through: One PC sees something in the sky. Nobody else reacts. A long black creature crawls out of a crack in reality. An Elsewhere predator. It begins chasing them. The PCs must finish the race while protecting classmates from a monster nobody else can perceive. They don't necessarily have to win. They just need telescope access somehow.

### The Telescope

The frame establishes the Ramparts telescope as a tool for observing nearby skylands—and spying on campus. At the precise atmospheric moment Riley calculated, they look through. The stars vanish. They see the Elsewhere. And Castle High exists there too. An inverted version.

Then someone says: _"Step away from the telescope."_ Dove.

### Dove Confesses

Dove finally breaks. Fellowes disappeared last year. The school board doesn't know. Parents don't know. Most faculty don't know. Dove covered it up. Not because Dove caused anything. Because they were terrified. Castle High had already lost eight students. If everyone learned it had also lost its headmaster, the school would probably be closed. Dove believes Vale knows what happened.

Before they can continue—Vale arrives. A spell. Dove drops unconscious. Vale takes Riley's map. Vale looks devastated rather than triumphant.

> _"I can bring them home."_

And flies toward Lock Tower.

## Session 6 — The Light in Lock Tower

### Purpose

Answer almost every mystery and force the PCs to decide whom they actually trust.

### Opening: Lockdown

Alarm bells. Faculty running everywhere. Lock Tower has opened for the first time anyone remembers. Dove is unconscious. Vale is missing. Naturally, every student is ordered into their dormitories. Naturally, the PCs do the opposite.

This is a great place to cash in relationships. Who helps them? The rival group? Marnie? A favorite teacher? A prefect they befriended? Someone they dated? Their relationships become resources.

### Entering Lock Tower

Make Lock Tower weird rather than simply dangerous. The first floor is an abandoned dormitory. Beds still made. Old magazines. Ancient graffiti. A centuries-old detention notice:

> **ELIAS FELLOWES — THREE DAYS DETENTION — UNAUTHORIZED SKYSHARD EXPERIMENTATION.**

The deeper they go, the older everything becomes. Eventually the architecture stops looking like a school. It becomes ancient.

### The Original Castle

They discover Castle High predates modern magical civilization. Its central skyshard is enormous. And the castle wasn't originally built to harness it. It was built to contain it.

The stained-glass words, mysterious stage-light patterns, strange catacomb noises, daily ashes in the gym—all those odd campus phenomena can become symptoms of the same ancient system. The frame deliberately seeds the campus with unexplained mysteries like these.

The school's founder tried to communicate through the skyshards. The experiment triggered the Rise. Or at least that's what the surviving records suggest; the frame itself deliberately leaves the true origin of skyshards and their activation unknown. The founder then built the containment mechanism and rewrote history.

Hence the graffiti: **LIAR.**

### Riley Appears

At the gateway chamber: Vale. A colossal skyshard. A shimmering tear. And Riley.

Riley is visible through it. They're alive. Fellowes is behind them. So are the other seven students. Then Riley explains. Oliver's escape weakened the seal. Riley discovered that someone needed to cross over temporarily to stabilize it. They went voluntarily. Vale didn't kidnap them. Vale desperately wants to rescue everyone.

But opening the portal completely will expose the world to something else. Something Riley has seen moving in the Elsewhere. **The Sleeper Above.**

### The Argument

Let this scene breathe.

> **Vale:** "They're children. We don't leave them there." **Fellowes:** "Opening this door could kill millions." **Riley:** "There has to be another way."

The PCs choose whom they believe. Then the Sleeper makes the choice irrelevant. A gigantic eye opens behind the Elsewhere castle. The portal tears wide. Castle High tilts. Books, furniture and people begin falling sideways.

Outside—The entire island starts dropping out of the sky.

_End session._

## Session 7 — Prom Night at the End of the World

### Purpose

Pay off everything: flight, school relationships, Riley, Vale, Fellowes, the tower, the PCs' growth, and their ability to solve problems adults couldn't. If possible, schedule the finale on Founder's Night/prom/homecoming. Because obviously the apocalypse happens while everyone is dressed up.

### Act I: Castle High Falls

Gravity is unstable. Hallways rotate ninety degrees. Windows shatter. Students fall toward the clouds. Teachers desperately evacuate classrooms. Elsewhere creatures crawl through dimensional tears.

Don't make the PCs immediately run toward the objective. First ask: _Who do you save?_ Give them several simultaneous emergencies. Their rival is dangling from a tower. Their favorite teacher is trapped. The griffin stable is collapsing. The dance floor has torn away. A younger student is falling. Make their relationships matter.

### Act II: Take Flight

The only route to the main skyshard is outside. Under the castle. This is the ultimate flight sequence.

The PCs launch from the Ramparts. Dive underneath Castle High. Fly between falling pieces of architecture. Avoid Elsewhere creatures. Use vents as acceleration tunnels. Ride magical updrafts. Catch each other when artifacts fail. Every PC gets one moment where their personal flying style is the thing that saves everyone. Their skyshard artifacts have been with them for the entire campaign. Now make them matter.

### Act III: The Elsewhere

They cross. Everything becomes silent. Castle High floats upside down beneath a black sun. The missing students are waiting. So is Fellowes. And something vast is approaching.

The Sleeper isn't a monster with hit points. It's practically a natural disaster. Fighting it directly is meaningless. The PCs need to solve the gateway problem.

Give them three obvious solutions:

1. **Seal the Door:** Safest for the world. But the people in the Elsewhere remain trapped. Possibly forever.
    
2. **Open the Door:** Bring everyone home immediately. But the Sleeper gets through too.
    
3. **Rewrite the System:** Dangerous. Nobody knows if it will work. Turn Castle High's central skyshard from a seal into a controlled bridge.
    

This should be the solution the adults dismissed as impossible. Because the PCs have spent seven sessions learning things from completely different fields. One character understands magical theory. One understands flight. One understands ancient ruins. One understands people. One remembers some stupid thing from chemistry class. And somehow: the homework matters. That's very Castle High.

### The Final Challenge

Require each PC to contribute something. Maybe their skyshard artifacts must temporarily become part of the network. Ask each player: _What memory from your first weeks at Castle High does your artifact hold onto?_

Riley laughing. Their first flight. A disastrous date. Their rival shaking their hand. Eating breakfast together. Getting detention. Whatever mattered. Those emotional connections stabilize the bridge.

Vale realizes someone must remain behind long enough to complete the transition. Don't automatically make Vale do it. Let the PCs talk. Maybe Fellowes volunteers. Maybe Vale does. Maybe the PCs invent another solution. Follow their choice.

### Escape

Then: The bridge activates. **RUN.**

Missing students race across. The Elsewhere collapses. The Sleeper reaches toward them. Everyone launches into one last aerial escape. Riley nearly falls. Someone catches them. Oliver, waiting on the other side, sees his friends coming home.

Castle High's skyshard ignites. The entire castle shoots upward through the clouds. Silence. Then gravity returns. Everyone falls approximately six feet onto the ruined dance floor.

## Epilogue — Monday

Give each PC one little epilogue. What happened with their crush? Did they reconcile with their rival? Did their grades survive? What do their parents think? Are they famous now? Did their tower win anything?

Then breakfast. The returned students are everywhere. Oliver is reunited with his friends. Fellowes has stepped down. Dove remains headmaster. Lock Tower is open. Professor Vale's fate depends on the PCs' choices.

Riley walks into breakfast. No dramatic entrance. They just sit down. Steal food from someone's plate.

> _"You guys did my homework while I was gone, right?"_

PA system:

> _"Would the members of [PC tower group] please report to Headmaster Dove's office immediately. ... Regarding approximately two hundred and seventeen violations of school policy."_

Everyone freezes. Riley looks toward the door. One PC's broom is leaning against the table. Riley grins.

> _"Race you."_

And they're gone.

## The Recurring Structure at the Table

There's one other adjustment to make when actually running these seven sessions: **Every episode should contain at least one completely mundane teenage problem that has nothing to do with the conspiracy.**

These characters may eventually become heroes, but right now they're students who have papers due, relationships falling apart, teachers to impress, and classes to pass. Loosely pair each adventure plot with a school plot:

|Session|Mystery / Adventure|Teenage Problem|
|---|---|---|
|**1**|Oliver returns; Riley disappears.|New roommates, rivals, orientation drama.|
|**2**|Debris field and dragonshark.|Detention and damaged tower grade.|
|**3**|Ghost and forbidden corridors.|Homework, secrets, rivalry.|
|**4**|Happyland and Elsewhere vision.|Illegal party, crushes, relationships.|
|**5**|Elsewhere predator.|Castle Cup and tower pride.|
|**6**|Lock Tower and gateway.|Friends choosing whether to help the PCs.|
|**7**|Save Castle High.|Dance night and saying what hasn't been said.|

That alternating rhythm is important. It means Session 4 isn't _"the Happyland dungeon"_; it's _"the night we snuck out to a party at an abandoned flying amusement park, somebody kissed their crush, and then we accidentally discovered our missing best friend in another dimension."_ That's much closer to the adventurous, dreamy, playful, melodramatic, full-hearted tone the campaign frame establishes.
